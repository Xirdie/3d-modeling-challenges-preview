6 classic animation exercises, from bouncing ball to walk cycle

The ladder every animation course starts with: bouncing ball (timing, spacing, squash and stretch), heavy vs light (weight), pendulum (follow-through), flour sack (anticipation), walk cycle (rhythm and arcs), head turn (overlap and a blink). Playblast to MP4 or a PNG sequence from any program, check your timing on free contact sheets, then get a graded review with notes on what to fix next.

  1. Bouncing ball: three bounces with decay, squash and stretch. Timing and spacing in their purest form.
  2. Heavy vs light ball: a bowling ball and a beach ball dropped side by side. Weight told through timing alone.
  3. Pendulum follow-through: a swinging pendulum with a trailing tail. Overlapping action and drag.
  4. Flour sack jump: anticipation, a jump and a landing with no face or limbs. Pose and weight shift.
  5. Walk cycle: a 24-frame side-view walk. Contact, down, passing and up poses, and clean arcs.
  6. Head turn with anticipation: a head turn with a blink. Anticipation, eased timing and overlap.

How each exercise is checked

Drop your playblast and the page samples 12 frames, at the challenge's key-pose times where it defines them, into three contact sheets and four full-size key poses. The frame count and frame rate are measured by code for free. Timing, spacing, arcs and weight are graded by AI in the full review, against the rubric published on each challenge page.

Playblast settings that work in a browser

Use an H.264 MP4 or a numbered PNG sequence at the challenge's frame rate. Maya's default AVI or QuickTime playblasts and HEVC or ProRes files don't open in browsers; set Format to image and Encoding to png instead. Each challenge page has the steps for Blender, Maya and 3ds Max.
