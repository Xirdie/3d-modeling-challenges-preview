3D modeling practice with instant graded feedback

A human critique costs $45-$250 and forum feedback can take weeks. Here your file stays in your browser: it renders a turntable, a wireframe and a silhouette and measures your topology for free; an AI review (labelled as AI, Claude by Anthropic) grades silhouette, proportion and brief compliance against a written rubric for $9, or 60 a month for $19. Edge flow is noted, not scored. It is not a replacement for a mentor: it is a fast first pass, every day.

What you get for free

  • A clay turntable you can rotate, a polygon wireframe that keeps your quads, and your silhouette beside the challenge's reference.
  • Measured counts: tris, quads, n-gons, poles, non-manifold and open edges, and size against the spec, scored as the "Topology, measured" rubric line.
  • One quick review a day: a grade band, two things that work and the one biggest fix, from three low-res views (Claude Haiku 5.5).

What the full review adds

Every rubric line graded from seven full-resolution snapshots by Claude Sonnet 5.5: what met the brief, what missed, and two or three concrete steps to move each line up. The letter, the score out of 100 and PASS or NOT YET are computed by code from the line scores, so the same marks always give the same grade. Your progress stays in your browser, with a grade card and a Markdown copy of the review to keep.

What it is not

It is an automated review against a published rubric, not a human critique and not a qualification. It reads rendered snapshots, so it judges what the camera sees: proportion, silhouette, shading and the brief. Topology numbers come from code, never from the AI.
