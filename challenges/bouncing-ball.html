Bouncing ball

Beginner MP4 / PNG sequence Animation

Done? Check your file The check is free; the full review is $9.

The brief

Animate a ball dropping from the top of the frame and bouncing three times, losing height on every bounce until it settles or rolls out.

Show the physics: the ball slows into each apex and speeds into each contact, squashes on contact and stretches on the fast frames around it, and its path makes clean, decaying arcs.

Keep the camera still and side-on, the ball fully in frame, 48 to 96 frames at 24 fps. Playblast to MP4 (H.264) or a PNG sequence.

Spec

  • Length: 48-96 frames at 24 fps (2-4 s)
  • Three bounces, each lower than the last
  • Squash on contact, stretch on the fast frames
  • Still, side-on camera; ball fully in frame
  • Export: H.264 MP4 or a PNG sequence

How to export

Blender

  1. Output Properties: Frame Rate 24 fps, Frame Start and End set to your shot.
  2. Output: File Format FFmpeg Video, Container MPEG-4, Video Codec H.264. Or File Format PNG for a frame sequence.
  3. Look through your side camera, then View > Viewport Render Animation.

Then drop the file below.

Maya

  1. Set the scene to 24 fps (Preferences > Settings > Working Units > Time: 24 fps) and the Time Slider to your shot.
  2. Windows > Playblast options: Format image, Encoding png. QuickTime and AVI playblasts don't open in browsers.
  3. Playblast from your side camera, then drop all the PNG frames together.

Then drop the file below.

3ds Max

  1. Set Time Configuration to 24 fps and the range to your shot.
  2. Tools > Preview - Grab Viewport > Create Preview Animation, or Render Setup > Common: Range, Render Output saved as PNG.
  3. Drop the PNG sequence (all frames at once).

Then drop the file below.

Other

  1. Any tool: export an H.264 MP4 or a numbered PNG sequence (ball_0001.png, ball_0002.png ...).
  2. 24 fps, a still side-on camera, the ball fully in frame.

Then drop the file below.
